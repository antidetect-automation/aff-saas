Multilogin vs AdsPower

Most “X vs Y” pages in this niche are feature dumps. This one is closer to how operators argue in chats: what breaks, what you’re already trained on, and whether price still decides the vendor.

Short version up front, because you didn’t come for suspense. If you write scripts against a browser API and you care about the accounts, I’d still put Multilogin first. If your team already lives inside AdsPower’s RPA flows and your spreadsheet is only about seats, staying put can be rational. Fresh start in 2026? Multilogin’s free five-profile tier and cheaper Pro yearly made the old “too rich to trial” excuse a lot weaker.

Where the products feel different day to day

AdsPower grew up as a volume workstation. Lots of people learned it because the seat math was friendly and the built-in automation covered “click through this flow” without opening VS Code. That muscle memory is real. Porting a whole VA playbook overnight is usually a bad idea.

Multilogin’s center of gravity is different. Multilogin X, start/stop profiles, attach Playwright or Puppeteer over CDP, keep cookies and proxies aligned — that’s the happy path we document in the Playwright guide. When something fails, it’s often auth, debugger URL timing, or proxy geo — the boring stuff we also log under /errors/.

Fingerprints: I’m not going to pretend there’s a public lab that coronates a permanent winner every quarter. What I will say, as someone steering traffic toward Multilogin: on stricter targets, teams that already trusted Multilogin rarely switch away for quality reasons. They switch for price or for an RPA habit. Quality rarely gets worse after a Multilogin migration; cost and process sometimes do.

Cloud Phone and “one vendor”

If half your lanes are Android apps, Multilogin’s Cloud Phone SKU matters. AdsPower conversations stay mostly in browser-profile land. You can always duct-tape a separate cloud-phone vendor next to AdsPower — people do — but then you’re debugging two billing cycles and two support desks. Multilogin isn’t magic there either; it’s just fewer moving parts when MIN50 is on the table.

The price story people still repeat

“Multilogin is 10× AdsPower” was a useful meme when Multilogin only showed expensive paid tiers in screenshots. Look at the 2026 pricing page: Free forever with five profiles (no API; idle cleanup after seven days), Pro from roughly $7.08 / month on yearly billing at ten profiles ($85 / year on the card we captured), and Business when you need seats at scale. Our desk code SAAS50 can cut browser checkout further — verify the invoice, don’t take blog math to finance.

AdsPower can still win a pure seat auction at very high counts, especially with negotiated plans. I’m not going to invent their current price list here; it changes and I’m not their affiliate. Compare live quotes next to Multilogin’s cart. That’s the only honest way.

A decision rule that isn’t marketing

Take the next thirty days of work you already know you’ll do. If more than half of it is “script talks to browser,” start Multilogin Free, then Pro when you need API, and apply SAAS50 when you pay. If more than half is “human or RPA walks a GUI inside AdsPower,” calculate migration hours before you chase a coupon.

Neither brand makes you undetectable. Proxies, warmup, and how loud you are on the target still decide more than the logo on the dock.
